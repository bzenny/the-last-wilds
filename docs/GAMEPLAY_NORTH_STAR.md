# Gameplay North Star and Clarity Gate

## North Star
Every decision should make the player feel like a guardian of a living wilderness, defending its future against an expanding civilization.

## Player experience
The player observes an advancing human frontier, identifies why and where it threatens living habitats, deploys or redirects monsters, and chooses whether to deter, destroy, reclaim or adapt. Every action must have a legible territorial consequence.

## Design constraints
- Agency: meaningful choices with opportunity costs.
- Readability: visible intentions, threats, costs and outcomes.
- Consequence: world tiles and behaviors change because of actions.
- Adaptation: changing human strategies require different monster responses.
- Replayability: seeded variation and multiple viable strategies.
- Ethical nuance: humans have understandable needs, not a universally evil faction.

## First-session target
1. Player sees sanctuary, three guardian roles and approaching logger route.
2. Player learns how roads and logging reduce habitat health.
3. Player chooses where to position a defender.
4. Human unit responds visibly; territory changes.
5. Player spends Wild Energy on regrowth or defense.
6. The encounter resolves with readable victory or loss feedback.

## Clarity acceptance criteria before Q2 implementation
- A one-page explanation of player goal, core loop, win/loss and 3 meaningful choices.
- Paper prototype with a 10-minute scenario and turn-by-turn outcomes.
- Definition of movement, deployment, attack, human intent and ecological feedback.
- UI wireframe showing threat direction, sanctuary status, Wild Energy and habitat health.
- Distinct tactical roles for three monsters and three human units.
- Test script: at least five participants can describe what they are defending, why humans are expanding, and the result of their last action without coaching.
- Resolve confusion and lock v0.1 scope before building deeper AI/combat systems.

## Visual North Star
Gameplay first. Silhouette, terrain contrast, faction cues, telegraphed threats and ecological feedback outrank decorative detail. Prototype with placeholder shapes until information hierarchy passes usability checks.

## Engineering sequence
1. Confirm build CI.
2. Design paper prototype and UI wireframes.
3. Validate gameplay clarity.
4. Implement deterministic movement, combat, human expansion and habitat interactions.
5. Playtest and adjust.
6. Replace placeholders with cohesive pixel-art assets.

## Not yet authorized by this gate
Full combat/AI/ecology implementation; online mod browser; large content production.
